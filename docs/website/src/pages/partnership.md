## About Gala

Founded at the University of Michigan in 2016 and now based at the [University of Notre Dame Pulte Institute for Global Development](https://keough.nd.edu/about/faculty-staff-directory/rebecca-hardin/), Gala is an initiative exploring ways to make learning more grounded, dynamic, and collaborative. We steward an open platform and community ([www.learngala.com](https://www.learngala.com)) for collaboratively authoring, revising, and sharing open interactive learning modules and case studies. Though our roots are in Sustainability and Environmental education, our interdisciplinary community now spans users and partners from multiple fields and institutions across the globe. Mutual interest and a reputation for Open Educational Resources that flexibly and delightfully merge research, teaching, and practice, has animated our community for over a decade.

## Funded Partnerships and Independent Open Use

Anyone can use Gala's tools to create, read, or teach with modules. By default, these modules are published in an unbranded collection, the [Shared Cases Library](https://www.learngala.com/catalog/libraries/shared). All of our users contribute to our community, but many do not have funding available to support Gala monetarily. For unfunded projects and groups of module creators, we may create module libraries and provide support as time permits.

We ask that projects, organizations, and institutions with funding available (or who are in the process of applying for funding) build-in support to help sustain the platform and initiative. The chart and examples below outline some of the ways that we fit into projects. The cost of these engagements scales with the level and type of support from the Gala team, but we have provided some general estimates. 

Send us an email at [learngala@nd.edu](mailto:learngala@nd.edu) to discuss partnership.

## Scoping Engagements

| Scope | Minimal Support | Sustained Support | Advanced Support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Technical support** | As needed | As needed | As needed |
| **How-to guidance and training** | Initial kickoff; online guides | Ongoing hands-on training and instructional design support as needed | Custom training and materials developed |
| **Editorial support** | Limited | Copyediting support; light review from Gala team | Design editorial processes for module review; heavy input from Gala team as needed |
| **Evaluation** | General library and module usage statistics | Specific insights relying on module usage data | Design and manage research codesign processes |
| **Integrations & branding** | Branded libraries of modules | Support for curating modules on organizational websites | Build out custom integrations with other platforms |
| **Custom features** | – | – | New features or open tools created |
| **Cost** | $10k annually | &lt;$40k\* | &gt;$40k\* |

\* Varies with project scope and level of effort.

### Minimal Support

Independent Gala users are typically individuals or small groups of instructors experimenting with creating and using Open Educational Resources or adapting their own materials to share. Instructors may also use [module creation/adaptation as a pedagogical tool](https://docs.learngala.com/docs/teaching-strategies#building-student-module-authorship-into-a-course) in their classrooms.

<Example title="Student module authorship at the University of Michigan">

Perrin Selcer, of the University of Michigan, pioneered student module authorship with Gala as a pedagogical practice, [assigning his Environmental History students](https://lsa.umich.edu/history/news-events/all-news/facultynews/finding-balance-through-technology.html) to "synthesize scholarship on a well-known development project into a story that represented key lessons from the course."

</Example>

<Example title="Outbreaks! By NC State">

[Carlos Goller](https://bio.sciences.ncsu.edu/people/ccgoller/), of North Carolina State University, started the [*Outbreaks By NC State!* library](https://www.learngala.com/catalog/libraries/outbreaks-biotechnology-and-sustainability-at-nc-state), which accompanies his honors course, *Outbreak!* Students develop modules throughout the semester which are housed in this library. Students may develop novel modules or revise or remix existing ones from previous semesters, creating shared learning experiences across cohorts.

</Example>

### Sustained Support

Projects with this scope require more support and/or training from the Gala team, including things like copyediting, instructional design support, and module piloting and evaluation. This can go beyond the basic level, or even beyond conventional classrooms.

<Example title="Distributed solar energy harvesting for carbon-free ammonia synthesis">

Funded by the NSF EFRI D-CHEM program, this project used Gala for creating deliverables including a teaching case about the technologies being developed through the grant, and analysis of learning experiences for students producing modules in engineering courses about renewable energy.

</Example>

<Example title="CODOWN">

The [Consortium on Disinfection by-products and Opportunistic pathogens in Water Networks (CODOWN)](https://cfpub.epa.gov/ncer_abstracts/index.cfm/fuseaction/display.abstractDetail/abstract_id/11404/report/0) is an EPA-funded project that explores tradeoffs in drinking water treatment related to opportunistic pathogens and the production of disinfectant by-products. A goal of the project is to engage with water utilities and co-develop training or continuing education credits for and with utility professionals. The Gala team co-developed the [prototype module](https://www.learngala.com/catalog/libraries/co-down) and hosted revisioning sessions (called "networkshops"), which invited practicioners and researchers to contribute feedback. Gala has also presented this work [at conferences](https://opened26.sched.com/event/2RCVi) and co-authored academic papers on the project and its process.

</Example>

### Advanced Support

These projects require a higher degree of customization and may involve working with diverse networks of authors, codesign at various levels, project management and even building interoperability with other platforms. We join the project as an active partner, codesigning systems for module creation and use, and measuring impact. In the past, partners have opted to extend Gala rather than build out a new open platform, which has connected them to a wider community and allowed the impact of their contribution to last beyond the initial scope of the project.

<Example title="OCELOTS">

The [Online Content for Experiential Learning of Tropical Systems (OCELOTS)](https://ocelots.nrem.iastate.edu/) is an NSF-funded network that "brings together tropical biology researchers, active learning pedagogy specialists, software developers, and media specialists, with the vision of creating an open-access, online resource library of learning modules in tropical ecology." OCELOTS chose to use Gala to create and showcase their OER modules, while integrating it with another platform, [QUBESHub](https://qubeshub.org/), to host incubator and Faculty Mentoring Network sessions focusing on module creation and use. The collaboration began with the creation of an [Authoring Guidelines and Toolkits module](https://www.learngala.com/cases/ocelots-author-guidelines-toolkits), which brought the community together to define how OCELOTS modules would be created. The Gala team provided extensive instructional design, evaluation, and technical guidance, and co-authorship for several journal publications throughout.

> *How can educational networks bridge the researcher–educator–software developer gaps and systematically translate under-represented scientific research, such as tropical ecology, into immersive, interactive, and quantitative virtual active learning experiences without top-down institutional support? The innovation of OCELOTS to address this gap is related to its integrative use of a platform that facilitates a media-rich and interactive storytelling presentation (Gala, www.learngala.com) with the building of a community of people dedicated to creating, reviewing, and implementing modules.*
>
> — Russell, *et al.* ([Fostering a network for creating and implementing open education resources in ecology](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210x.70387))

</Example>

<Example title="SEEK Commons">

[NSF Socio-Environmental Knowledge (SEEK) Commons](https://seekcommons.org/) is an NSF Research Coordination Network focused on commoning and open science, and applied ideas from work with previous networks to translate workshops and deliverables from Fellows into dynamic learning modules. To integrate these deliverables into their CARE/FAIR-focused Resource Hub, Gala worked with the SEEK Commons team to extend Gala by [integrating it with the Wikidata knowledge graph](http://localhost:3001/blog/wikidata-links) via rich metadata. This work is reported on in a forthcoming book chapter.

</Example>
